# Internet Connectivity and Students' Learning Experience at NSUK

**NS-DTS 223 Data Exploration and Visualization, Group Practical Assignment (Topic 2)**
Nasarawa State University, Keffi, Department of Computer Science (Data Science)

An end-to-end exploratory data analysis of a student survey (n = 84, 18–22 Nov 2026) on campus internet connectivity and its relationship to students' learning. The workflow follows the assignment brief: **problem → data collection → cleaning → EDA → visualization → data story → recommendations**.

---

## Central question

> *How do students experience internet connectivity across the NSUK campus, and how is a poorer connectivity experience associated with disruption to their learning?*

| # | Research question | Visualization |
|---|---|---|
| RQ1 | How do students access the internet on campus? | V1: Bar charts (providers, connection type) |
| RQ2 | Does the connectivity experience differ across campus locations and providers? | V2: Box plot (CEI by location) |
| RQ3 | When is connectivity best? | V3: Line chart (best time of day) |
| RQ4 | Is poorer connectivity associated with greater academic impact? | V4: Heatmap (rating × impact) |
| RQ5 | Are students with poorer connectivity more likely to miss deadlines? | V5: Grouped bar chart |
| RQ6 | What do students want the university to do? | Supporting bar charts |

## Key findings

1. **Heavy dependence, self-funded access.** 89% use the internet *very often/always* for academic work. **82% rely on their own mobile data**, only 10% on campus Wi-Fi, and 65% spend over ₦3,000 per week on data.
2. **A middling experience everywhere.** The median Connectivity Experience Index is **44/100**. 52% experience poor connectivity *often/always*, and only 32% are satisfied with their provider's speed. Differences between locations and providers are **not statistically significant**: the problem is campus-wide.
3. **Best at night.** 45% of students who named a time say connectivity is best **after 8 PM**, and only 19% say the afternoon. This is consistent with daytime congestion.
4. **Linked to academic harm.** Poorer connectivity goes with greater perceived academic impact (Spearman ρ = −0.31, p = 0.005; still ρ = −0.28 after controlling for spend, level and usage). Students rating their connection *Poor* are **2.6× as likely** to report a high academic impact (65% vs 25%).
5. **Deadlines are missed widely.** **71%** have missed an academic deadline because of poor connectivity: 85% of *Poor*-rated, 72% of *Average* and 59% of *Good* (trend not significant).

**Recommendations:** campus-wide Wi-Fi in academic spaces with enough capacity for daytime peaks; capacity agreements with MTN, Glo and Airtel; teaching practices that keep working through outages (recordings, offline materials, deadline grace periods); zero-rated portal and e-library access; and regular objective speed testing.

## Key cleaning decisions

| Issue | Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Q8 recorded as text up to 20 Nov, then as digits 1–5 (31 rows) | Map 1 = Very poor … 5 = Excellent | Direction checked: numeric ratings correlate with the other items in the same direction as text ratings |
| Multiple ticks on single-choice Likert items (8 cells) | Adjacent options → the one nearer the midpoint; contradictory options → missing | Conservative; does not exaggerate either extreme |
| 27 department spellings, 6 blanks | Standardised to 19 names; blanks → "Not stated" | Case, spacing and abbreviation variants |
| Microbiology listed under Social Sciences (1 row) | Faculty corrected to NAS and flagged | Department is the more specific answer |
| "No noticeable difference" ticked with a specific time (4 rows) | Kept the specific time | More informative answer |
| Non-informative open text ("Yes", "Nil", "Non"…) | Flagged and excluded from theme coding | Not analysable |
| Missing answers (≤ 2 per item) | Kept; available-case analysis | No respondent dropped; each statistic reports its *n* |
| Outliers | None (fixed-option items, no CEI Tukey outliers); 1 contradictory pattern flagged and kept | No basis for removal |

**Connectivity Experience Index (CEI):** the mean of Q8 (rating), reversed Q9 (poor connectivity frequency), reversed Q11 (disconnection frequency) and Q12 (satisfaction), rescaled to 0–100. Cronbach's α = 0.78.

## Limitations

* n = 84 (below the 100 target), from a convenience sample dominated by NAS (79%) and 200–300 Level (87%) students. Results are indicative, not campus-wide estimates.
* All measures are self-reported. No speed tests or direct observation were carried out.
* The instrument changed during collection (Q8 format; checkboxes on single-choice items).
* Cross-sectional data show associations, not causes.
* About 20 significance tests were run; borderline p-values (0.05–0.08) are treated as hypotheses only.

## Data source and ethics

Primary data collected by the group with a Google Form, 18–22 November 2026. Participation was voluntary and anonymous, with no personally identifying information collected. The original response sheet is preserved unchanged in `data/raw/`.
